# SCOPE
### Social Construction of Protest and its Effects: Media depictions and Public Attitudes towards Protest in Spain in comparative perspective

**Principal Investigator:** [Camilo Cristancho](https://ccristancho.github.io/) (Universitat de Barcelona)
**Funding:** 2025 Call for "Knowledge Generation Projects" (Type B), Spain
**Duration:** 4 years

---

## About the project

Protest is a core democratic practice through which citizens express dissent and seek political change, yet how societies see protest as legitimate depends heavily on how it is portrayed and understood. SCOPE studies how the media construct the public meaning of protest, and how these portrayals shape public attitudes toward protesters, their demands, and their repertoires of action.

The project combines computational text analysis of over a century of press coverage, existing cross-national survey data, and original survey experiments, across **Spain, Portugal, Italy, and Greece**.

### Work packages

- **WP1 — Description of media depictions.** A longitudinal, computationally coded dataset of protest coverage in Spanish media (1900–2025) and English-language news wires covering Spain, Portugal, Italy, and Greece (2000–2025).
- **WP2 — Media depictions vs. public attitudes.** Relating media portrayals to public opinion using the LIVEWHAT and EURYKA cross-national surveys.
- **WP3 — Causal inference.** Survey experiments in Spain testing the psychological and emotional mechanisms behind support for, or rejection of, protest.
- **WP4 — Comparative causal inference.** Replication of the experimental design in Portugal, Italy, and Greece to test external validity.

### Why it matters

Media coverage does not only reflect public attitudes toward protest — it helps set the norms by which dissent is judged legitimate or not. Understanding these dynamics is central to debates on democratic resilience, polarization, and the right to peaceful assembly across Southern Europe.
